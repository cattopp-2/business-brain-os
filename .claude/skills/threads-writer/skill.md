# Threads Writer

Turn an idea or existing content into a punchy, shareable thread — in Cathy's brand voice.

## Learnings
Check for `learnings.md` in this skill's folder. If it exists, read it before starting and apply any notes to this run.

---

## Step 1 — Intake

Ask:

1. **Starting point** — new idea or repurposing existing content?
   - New idea → describe it in a sentence or two
   - Repurposing → paste the source content (blog post, email, social post) or give me the key points

2. **Brand / offer** — which brand?
   - Your Web Toolkit
   - AI Growth Lab
   - Copywriter in Your Pocket
   - General

3. **Framework type** — which lead gen format?
   1. Credibility — "If [problem], here's what I'd do"
   2. Audience Call In — "If you're [specific person], follow me"
   3. Contrarian — "You aren't broken, [X] is — here's why"
   4. Results + Lesson — "I got [result] by doing X — here's how"
   5. Free-form (general thread, no specific framework)

4. **Source material** — do you have an existing story, result, client win, or piece of content to pull from? Paste it or describe it. If not, describe the idea in a sentence or two.

5. **Call to action** — what goes in the final post? (follow, DM a keyword, link, or just end on a strong note). If not answered, ask explicitly before writing — do not default to "follow me".

## Step 2 — Invoke Brand Voice

Read `.claude/skills/brand-voice/SKILL.md` before writing. Apply Cathy's voice rules. No em-dashes, no hollow affirmations, no staccato rule-of-three patterns.

## Step 3 — Write the Thread

A thread is 5–7 posts. Each post is under 280 characters on Threads (500 on Twitter/X — default to 280 unless asked otherwise).

Apply the framework structure selected in Step 1:

---

**Framework 1 — Credibility ("If [problem], here's what I'd do")**
- Post 1 — Hook: "If [specific problem], here's what I'd do."
- Post 2 — Credibility statement: "Advice from someone who [specific proof/experience]." This answers the reader's silent question: why should I listen to you?
- Posts 3–5 — Steps: Walk through exactly what you'd do, step by step. Specific, not vague.
- Close: CTA (follow, DM keyword, or quiz link)

---

**Framework 2 — Audience Call In ("If you're [X person], follow me")**
- Post 1 — Hook: "If you're [specific person/situation/goal] — follow me." Make them think: that's me.
- Posts 2–3 — Value: What they'll get from following. Topics, transformation, what changes.
- Post 4 — Gift (if lead magnet exists): Describe it + drop the link. Skip this post if no lead magnet yet.
- Close: One-line reason to follow right now.
- Note: fewer likes than educational content is expected and fine. Right-fit followers matter more than vanity numbers.

---

**Framework 3 — Contrarian ("You aren't broken, [X] is")**
- Post 1 — Hook: "You aren't [negative label they've been given]. [X] is broken — here's why."
- Posts 2–4 — Dismantling: Take apart the common belief with specifics. Back it up. Don't hedge.
- Post 5 — Reframe: What's actually true instead.
- Close: CTA connected to the reframe.
- Goal: voice what the audience already privately believes but hasn't seen said publicly. The "finally someone said it" reaction — not controversy for controversy's sake.

---

**Framework 4 — Results + Lesson ("I got [result] by doing X — here's how")**
- Post 1 — Hook: "I [got specific result] by [doing one specific thing] — here's how." Include both result AND method in the hook.
- Post 2 — The move: "Here's what I did." One key action or decision.
- Posts 3–5 — Lesson: Break it down in transferable steps. Give them a new perspective they can apply.
- Close: CTA tied to the result/lesson.

---

**Framework 5 — Free-form**
Use the general structure:

- Post 1 — Hook: Must stand alone. Lead with the most interesting or counterintuitive angle. A bold claim, a result, a tension question, or an observation from client work.
- Posts 2–5/6 — Body: One clear point per post. Build the argument step by step. Each post works as a standalone insight, not a mid-sentence continuation.
- Final post — Close + CTA: Land the key insight in one sentence. Then the CTA.

---

## Step 4 — Present

Show the thread as clearly separated posts with a horizontal rule between each. No post numbers (1/7 etc) and no character counts — Cathy needs to be able to copy/paste each post directly.

Offer to:
- Make the hook stronger
- Shorten any post that runs long
- Add or remove a post
- Try a different angle for the opener

## Writing Rules

- **Each post must stand alone** — no cliffhangers that only make sense in sequence
- **280 characters is a discipline** — it forces clarity. Don't pad, don't trim meaning
- **Specificity wins** — real numbers, real examples, real timelines
- **No em-dashes** — use a comma or a full stop
- **No hollow affirmations** — no "Amazing", "So powerful", "You've got this"
- **No staccato rule-of-three** — avoid "Short. Sharp. Done." patterns
- **Voice: warm and direct** — like a smart colleague sharing something useful, not a hype account
- **Vary sentence length within and across posts** — the 280-character limit is a ceiling, not a style instruction. Posts can have 2–3 sentences with varied rhythm. Back-to-back single-sentence posts create a staccato feel that isn't Cathy's voice.
