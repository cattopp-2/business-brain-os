# Story Researcher

Research story ideas across 4 angles → present 6–10 hooks for approval.

These are raw material to draw on when writing, not finished drafts.

---

## Step 1 — Intake

Ask:

1. **Topic or theme** — what are we researching? (e.g. "selling without feeling pushy", "why business owners resist using AI", "building an audience from scratch") — even a rough direction works
2. **Anything to avoid?** — optional: angles already well-covered, tones that feel off, topics that have been done to death

If a topic is already in `$ARGUMENTS`, extract and confirm it — skip question 1.

Work with whatever you get. Even a rough topic is enough to start.

---

## Step 2 — Research (all 4 types before showing anything)

Run searches across all 4 story types. Use multiple queries per type. Do not show partial results mid-research — wait until all searches are complete before presenting anything.

### Story Type 1: Adjacent Industry Stories

Stories from completely different fields — sport, healthcare, hospitality, military, aviation, education — that mirror or illuminate the topic.

Query patterns (adapt to the topic):
- `[topic keyword] lesson from sport OR coach OR athlete`
- `[topic keyword] lesson from doctor OR hospital OR healthcare`
- `[topic keyword] lesson from restaurant OR hotel OR hospitality`
- `[topic keyword] military OR aviation parallel [current year]`
- `story from [unrelated field] that teaches [topic keyword]`

**What to look for:** Concrete, nameable stories — a specific team, a specific moment, a documented event — that can be retold briefly and connected back to the topic.

---

### Story Type 2: News and Trend Hooks

Current events, cultural moments, or emerging trends the topic can be tied to.

Query patterns:
- `[topic keyword] trend 2025 OR 2026`
- `[topic keyword] news [current month] [current year]`
- `what is changing about [topic keyword] [current year]`
- `[topic keyword] shifting [current year]`

**What to look for:** Things happening RIGHT NOW — not evergreen. A news story, a cultural moment, a shift in behaviour or attitude that the audience would recognise.

---

### Story Type 3: Research-Backed Stories

Statistics, studies, data, or research findings that could anchor a narrative.

Query patterns:
- `[topic keyword] research study statistics`
- `[topic keyword] data report [current year]`
- `[topic keyword] psychology OR behavioural science study`
- `surprising statistics about [topic keyword]`
- `counterintuitive finding about [topic keyword] research`

**What to look for:** Specific numbers, named studies, counterintuitive findings. A percentage, a university study, an industry report. These are hooks because they give Cathy a credible fact to open with — "A study found that X..." or "According to [source], Y% of..."

---

### Story Type 4: Real-World Examples

Documented case studies, business examples, or public stories of the topic playing out.

Query patterns:
- `[topic keyword] case study example`
- `[topic keyword] success story small business OR entrepreneur`
- `famous example of [topic keyword] that worked OR failed`
- `[topic keyword] before and after result`
- `[topic keyword] entrepreneur OR business owner story`

**What to look for:** Nameable, verifiable examples — a company, a person, a well-documented result. Not vague generalisations.

---

## Step 3 — Synthesise and Present 6–10 Story Hooks

After all searches are complete, synthesise the best findings into a numbered table. Aim for variety: at least one story from each type, no more than 3 from any single type.

Present in this format:

| # | Story Hook | Type | Source | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Opening line written as Cathy would open a post] | Adjacent Industry | [Source note] | [One sentence on the fit] |
| 2 | ... | News/Trend | ... | ... |

**The Story Hook column is the most important one.** Write it as a usable opening line or story setup — not a topic label.

- Not: "Sports parallel to selling"
- Yes: "The All Blacks don't call it 'selling' — they call it 'standards'. Here's what their pre-match ritual taught me about following up with clients."

- Not: "Research on email open rates"
- Yes: "A Stanford study found that people make up their minds about whether to trust someone within 7 seconds. Your subject line is your 7 seconds."

After the table:

> "Which of these do you want to keep? Give me the numbers — or tell me to tweak any of them first."

Wait for the response before doing anything else.

---

## Step 4 — Handle Tweaks (if requested)

If any hook needs adjusting, update the wording and confirm the final version before finishing.

---

## Notes

- **Run all 4 search types every time** — don't skip a type because the initial searches look thin. Broaden the query.
- **The hook is the brief** — write it as a usable post opener, not a description of what the post is about.
- **UK spelling throughout** — favour, behaviour, organisation, etc.
