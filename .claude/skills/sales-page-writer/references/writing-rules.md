# Sales Page Writing Rules

Apply these rules to every sales page regardless of offer type.

## Formatting
- Short paragraphs — 2–3 sentences maximum in most sections
- Lots of white space — put sentences on new lines where it aids readability
- Active voice throughout
- First person: use "I" not "we" (unless the client specifically uses "we")
- Section labels in templates (e.g. "Problem Block", "Transformation Block", "Offer Introduction") are internal structure markers — do not output them as visible headings in the final page. Only the cross-headlines within each section appear in the copy.

## Language
- Specific over vague — always. Name real situations, real outcomes, real numbers
- No false urgency or pressure tactics — if there's genuine scarcity, name it plainly
- No em-dashes — use a comma, full stop, or rewrite the sentence instead
- No rule-of-three staccato sentence cadence — use commas to connect ideas, or move examples into bullets
- Don't start paragraphs with short validating phrases before getting to the point
- Don't start sentences with a short phrase and a colon
- No emojis
- No hashtags

## Words & Structures to Avoid
- Transform / Transformative / Elevate / Unlock / Game-changer / Empower / Supercharge
- "No fluff" / "pulling back the curtain" / "I get it" / "you're not alone"
- "Here's the deal" / "Here's the thing" / "Clear the fog" / "Hamster wheel"
- "It isn't this… it's this" / "It's not just… it's a"
- "Say goodbye to… say hello to"
- "Most coaches I speak to…" / "Many business owners…"

## CTAs
- Every CTA must have action + outcome: not just "Join" but "Join Now" or "Become A Member"
- One CTA mechanic per page — don't mix "DM me", "click here", and "book a call" on the same page

## Placeholders
- Anything still needed from the user must be clearly flagged: [PLACEHOLDER — describe what's needed]
- Never leave an unmarked gap in the copy
