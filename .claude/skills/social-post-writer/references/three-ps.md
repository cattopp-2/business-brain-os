# Framework: The 3Ps — Perspective Pivot With Proof

Acknowledges a common belief in the audience, then guides them to a new perspective using your expertise and proof. Combines empathy, education, and authority-building. The proof element (a real client story or data) is what makes this land — it's not just opinion, it's evidence.

## The Framework

1. Identify the seed idea — a belief or assumption common in your audience
2. Acknowledge why they hold that belief (show you understand)
3. Introduce the new belief — what they should actually believe
4. Establish why they can trust you on this (your expertise, background, track record)
5. Share proof — a specific client story or example that demonstrates the new belief in action
6. CTA

## Writing Notes

- The seed idea should be stated plainly — something the audience would say themselves
- The empathy section is brief — validate, don't over-explain
- Trust establishment is about relevant credentials, not a full bio
- The proof story should be specific with real outcomes
- CTA should feel like a natural invitation, not a hard pivot

## Example

"I need to earn more money to achieve financial freedom."

I understand why you'd think this. More income feels like the most direct route to fewer worries.

But true financial freedom isn't primarily about how much you make. It's about how you manage what you have.

In 15 years as a financial advisor, I've seen clients with six-figure incomes drowning in debt, and clients with modest salaries building real wealth. The difference is always strategy, not salary.

Here's an example that illustrates this. Two clients came to me at the same time. John was earning £150,000 a year and living paycheck to paycheck, with significant credit card debt. Sarah was earning £60,000. We worked with both for two years.

With John, we focused on a strict budget, paying down high-interest debt, and adjusting his lifestyle to live below his means. With Sarah, we implemented careful budgeting, investment in low-cost index funds, and maximising her employer pension match.

Two years later, John had eliminated his credit card debt but was still far from financial freedom. Sarah had built a full emergency fund, was maxing out her retirement contributions, and had started a side business.

It's not about how much you earn. It's about what you do with it.
