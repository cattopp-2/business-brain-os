# Framework: Future Vision

Paints a specific, desirable picture of what's possible, then provides concrete steps to bridge the gap between now and that future. Aspirational but grounded — the vision should feel achievable, not fantastical.

## The Framework

1. Paint a vivid, specific picture of a desired outcome
2. Acknowledge where they likely are now
3. Share 3 practical steps toward that vision
4. Introduce your offer as the bridge
5. Clear CTA

## Writing Notes

- The opening vision should be specific and sensory — not "imagine being successful" but "imagine opening your laptop to three new enquiries from ideal clients"
- Don't linger too long on the current pain — this framework is about the future, not the problem
- The three steps should be real and implementable — not vague advice
- Include proof of results if possible before the CTA

## Example

Imagine opening your laptop each morning to find three new enquiries from people who already understand your value and are ready to invest in your expertise.

Your social media isn't a constant demand for attention — it's a system that attracts the right people while you focus on serving the ones you already have. You're not working more hours. The right people are simply finding you more often.

I've helped dozens of service-based business owners get to this point, and it consistently comes down to three things.

First, being in the right places with the right message. Not everywhere — the specific platforms where your ideal clients actually spend their time. For one of my clients, that meant dropping Twitter entirely and focusing on LinkedIn and email. Her qualified leads doubled within two months.

Second, a content approach that multiplies rather than exhausts. One piece of well-made content turned into multiple formats without increasing the workload. A recent client built three months of content in a single afternoon using this method.

Third, a filter that qualifies leads before they reach your inbox. When your messaging is clear enough, only the right people raise their hands — and you spend your discovery calls converting, not sorting.

The result is a marketing system that keeps working when you're not at your desk.
