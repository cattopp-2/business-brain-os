# Framework: Reading Their Minds

Demonstrates deep empathy by setting a scene the audience is living in right now. Introduces their problem, shows you understand exactly how it's showing up in their daily life, then shares your solution. When done well, the audience feels like you're describing their Tuesday afternoon.

## The Framework

1. Set the scene — a specific, relatable moment already happening in their day
2. Name the problem
3. Show you understand how it's showing up in their life (the detail, the feeling, the cost)
4. Share your solution

## Writing Notes

- The scene-setting is everything — make it time-specific, location-specific, and sensory where possible
- "It's 6am and your alarm goes off" is better than "you're struggling with motivation"
- The "show you understand" section is where empathy lives — be specific about the daily reality, not abstract about the struggle
- The solution section should feel like relief, not a pitch
- Keep the CTA soft — this framework builds trust, it shouldn't feel like a close

## Examples

**Example 1: Fitness Coach**

It's 6am. Your alarm goes off. You groan and hit snooze for the third time this week.

You promised yourself you'd start that new workout routine, but your bed is very comfortable and the day ahead is already full.

You want to get fit, but finding the motivation to exercise consistently feels impossible.

You drag yourself out of bed, feeling guilty and frustrated. You catch a glimpse of yourself in the mirror as you rush through your morning. You know you should be exercising — but between work, family, and everything else, it just seems too hard to fit in without something else suffering.

What if there was a way to get fit that doesn't require hours at the gym or waking up before it's light? My Fit in 15 programme is built for people exactly like you. Just 15 minutes a day, at a time that works for your life, building a body you feel good in. No guilt, no all-or-nothing thinking — just results.

**Example 2: Tech VA**

It's late afternoon. You're staring at your screen surrounded by Post-it notes and half-finished to-do lists. Your inbox is full, your calendar is a mess, and you've just remembered an important client meeting you forgot to prepare for.

As a small business owner, you're drowning in admin that keeps pulling you away from the work that actually grows your business.

You feel stretched too thin. You know you should be working on strategy and client delivery — but instead you're spending hours on scheduling, data entry, and email management. Hiring a full-time assistant feels financially out of reach. But you can't keep going like this either.

Imagine having a skilled professional handle all those time-consuming tasks so you can focus on what actually matters. As a Tech VA, I take the administrative weight off your plate — inbox management, process automation, the lot — so you have the time and mental space to grow the thing you built. All virtual, no overhead.

Interested? Let's have a chat about what that could look like for you.
