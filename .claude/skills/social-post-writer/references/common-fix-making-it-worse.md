# Framework: The Common Fix That's Making It Worse

Names the popular industry-wide advice, trend, or tool that everyone's using — then explains why it's backfiring. Different from "What Have They Tried Before" which is about the individual's personal journey. This framework is about calling out the collective myth or trend.

Builds instant rapport by validating that the audience's frustration with standard advice is legitimate, not a personal failure.

## The Framework

1. Call out the go-to fix people rely on — the trend, tool, or tip everyone's using
2. Explain why it sounds good in theory but causes problems in practice
3. Share a short story or client example showing the failure (and the turnaround)
4. Offer your method as the approach that avoids this pitfall
5. CTA

## Writing Notes

- The "common fix" must be genuinely common — something the audience will immediately recognise
- Don't be dismissive of people who've tried it — be honest about why it's appealing
- The client story should be specific and grounded
- Your method should feel like relief, not superiority

## Examples

**Example 1: Health Coach**

You've probably tried cutting carbs. Or skipping meals. Or downloading one of those apps that tells you bananas are basically sugar bombs.

And for a minute, it kind of worked. Until your energy crashed, the cravings kicked in, and one off day turned into a weekend, followed by a fresh wave of guilt and a new diet plan on Monday.

The quick-fix approach to weight loss isn't just unhelpful — it's messing with your metabolism, your mood, and your mindset.

One of my clients came to me after six rounds of a highly restrictive programme. She could recite every calorie in every meal, but couldn't remember the last time she ate without anxiety.

So we did something different. We stopped food tracking and focused on nourishment. On learning how her body actually felt before and after meals. Within weeks, her energy stabilised, her clothes fit better, and the mental drama around food was gone.

If you're sick of plans that only work when you're being perfect, there's another way. DM me and I'll tell you more about how I help people get real, lasting results without starting over every Monday.

**Example 2: Business Consultant**

Everyone tells you to post every day. Reels. Lives. Carousels. Email. More. More. More.

But what no one tells you is that showing up constantly without a strategy is one of the fastest ways to burn out — and disappear altogether.

You don't need more content. You need a system.

One of my clients was posting daily and nothing was landing. No comments, no sales, just a full calendar and a sinking feeling that maybe it wasn't working.

So we scrapped the content calendar and built three core content pillars around her actual offers. Mapped those into a repeatable three-week cycle. Now she's posting half as often, booking more calls, and has her weekends back.

If the pressure to be everywhere is making you want to be nowhere, let's talk. I'll show you a smarter way to create content that works without eating your life.
