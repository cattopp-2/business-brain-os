# Framework: Story-Led Post

Opens mid-scene in a specific real moment — already happening, not explained. Lets the story breathe before pivoting to the insight or offer. The emotional truth of the story does the work. The connection to the offer or insight arrives late and feels earned.

Different from Success Story (which is about a client result) and Sweet Spot Story (which explicitly frames your experience against theirs) — this is more literary and voice-led. No hard sell. The story is the point.

## The Framework

1. Open mid-scene in a specific real moment — no preamble, already in it
2. Let the story breathe for several paragraphs before pivoting
3. Use "~" as a scene break if moving between sections
4. Land the insight or connection to the offer late — it should feel earned, not signposted
5. CTA is casual and low-pressure, or absent entirely

## Writing Notes

- The opening moment should be specific and real — a place, a thing, a time
- Don't explain the relevance upfront — let it emerge
- Most paragraphs carry 2–3 sentences that build before a short line lands — avoid fragmenting every thought into its own line
- The "~" scene break earns its place when there's a genuine shift in time, place, or perspective
- If there's a CTA, it should feel like an invitation, not a close

## Example

There's a voice memo on my phone from about three months ago. I recorded it while walking the dog, something about a content idea I thought was genuinely good. I remember thinking "I'll sort that later."

I have never found that idea again.

Not because it didn't get saved. It's there. Buried somewhere between a password I said out loud so I wouldn't forget it and an audio message I sent myself about a client project that probably resolved itself.

For years I had the same problem with notes, links, screenshots of things I meant to read. All captured. Completely unfindable.

~

What I've built now is a Claude skill that does the filing for me. I put something in and it gets sorted, tagged, and connected to related things I've already captured. The voice memo from dog walking goes in the same place as the half-finished note about the same client, because it knows they're related.

The magic isn't that it stores more. It's that I've stopped being the person who has to organise it all.

I just capture and trust it's findable.
