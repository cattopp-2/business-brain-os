# Framework: Epiphany (The Story Spine)

Builds brand authenticity and connection by sharing a personal transformation story — how your journey shaped the business you've created. Based on the Pixar Story Spine framework.

**IMPORTANT — how to use this framework:**
The Story Spine gives you the SHAPE of the narrative, not the words. Do NOT write the literal phrases "Once upon a time...", "Every day...", "But one day...", "Because of that...", "Until finally..." in the post. Those are scaffolding, not copy.

Write in Cathy's natural voice — flowing paragraphs that follow the arc without using the scaffold language. The examples below show the right approach.

## The Story Arc (shape, not literal language)

**The before state** — What life or business looked like before. The daily routine, the struggle, what was normal but not working.

**The discovery moment** — The thing that changed everything. A realisation, a stumble, an accident, a moment of clarity.

**The cascade** — What happened as a result of that moment. Usually two or three "because of that" turns — each one building on the last toward something bigger.

**The new reality** — Where things landed. What's possible now that wasn't before.

**The bridge to the offer** — How this journey directly connects to what you now help others do.

## Writing Notes

- This is a personal story — it should sound like Cathy talking, not a case study
- The "before state" should feel relatable to the audience's current situation
- The discovery moment doesn't need to be dramatic — it can be small and specific
- The cascade should feel like a natural domino effect, not a highlight reel
- End with a clear connection between the story and the offer

## Example 1 (Virtual Assistant Agency)

I was a stressed-out corporate executive, drowning in endless emails, missed deadlines, and a to-do list that multiplied overnight.

Every day I arrived before sunrise, gulped down coffee, and dived headfirst into a mess of client calls, team projects, and admin that never seemed to shrink. By the time I dragged myself home, long after dark, I felt like I'd run a marathon without moving an inch. Weekends were just extensions of the workweek — time to catch up on what I couldn't finish during the "normal" hours.

One lunchtime, scrolling my phone instead of eating, I came across an article about virtual assistants. It was like a switch flicked. Here were people who specialised in exactly the tasks that were eating my time and energy. I realised if I had someone like that, I could focus on the work that actually needed me.

So I hired one. My inbox became manageable. My calendar organised. My to-do list actually got shorter. I left the office at a reasonable hour and remembered what weekends were for.

That's when the second thing hit me. If I — a seasoned executive — could change so much with the right support, how many others were stuck the same way I had been?

I left my corporate job and built my own VA agency. A team of exceptional people, proper systems, a business model designed to actually scale. We started working with entrepreneurs, executives, and small business owners facing the same overwhelm I used to carry.

Our clients are achieving more, stressing less, and finding their feet again. And I've never felt more like I'm in the right place.

If you're buried under tasks that aren't really yours to carry, let's talk.

## Example 2 (Oven Cleaning Business)

Back in late 2019, I was all set to retrain as a plumber. I'd saved up, I'd done the research, I was ready for a change.

Then March 2020 happened.

With training programmes shut down and savings slowly disappearing, I started looking for anything I could do with my hands, from home. I remembered how satisfying it was to clean my own oven — genuinely therapeutic, the kind of task where you can see exactly what you've done.

I figured other people probably hated doing it. So I bought some equipment and started knocking on doors.

The demand was immediate. People were at home more, cooking more, and their ovens were suffering for it. What started as something to get through a difficult few months became something I actually looked forward to.

I took it seriously. Better equipment, refined techniques, wider marketing. Four years later I'm booked out weeks in advance and thinking about bringing on a team.

A pandemic and a dirty oven led me to my actual calling. Life's funny that way.

If your oven needs some attention, book early — this accidental entrepreneur's diary fills up fast.
