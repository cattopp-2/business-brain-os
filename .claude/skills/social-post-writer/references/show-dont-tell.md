# Framework: Show Don't Tell

Identifies a common misconception or belief in the audience, then shifts their perspective through empathy, education, and a demonstration of your methodology. Moves from empathy to education to practical insight to CTA.

The "soft teach" element is what makes this different from other belief-shifting frameworks — you show a piece of your actual method, not just assert that your approach is better.

## The Framework

1. Name the seed idea — a belief or misconception common in the audience
2. Acknowledge why they believe it (empathise)
3. Explain what they should believe instead
4. Soft teach — show a piece of your mechanism or method that illustrates the new belief
5. Reinforce the main message
6. CTA

## Writing Notes

- The seed idea should be something the audience would recognise and nod at
- The empathy step is brief — acknowledge, don't dwell
- The soft teach is the most valuable part — give real, specific detail from your method
- Reinforce at the end without restating the whole argument — one clear line
- Keep the CTA simple and low-pressure

## Example

"I need to cut out all carbs to lose weight."

I understand why you'd think this. Low-carb diets have a lot of success stories, and the logic seems solid.

But not all carbs are the same, and some are actually essential for a sustainable diet that supports weight loss.

Here's what we focus on in my nutrition practice instead. We look at the glycaemic index of foods and find practical swaps — so instead of white bread, you're eating wholegrain alternatives that keep your blood sugar stable and reduce cravings. We also time carbohydrate intake around your activity levels, which means your body is actually using what you eat rather than storing it. The result is gradual, sustainable progress rather than the restriction-rebound cycle most people get stuck in.

It's not about eliminating carbs. It's about choosing the right ones at the right time.
